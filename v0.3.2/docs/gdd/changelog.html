5. Changelog ​

Every published version stays playable at its own URL. The landing page lists them all.

v0.3.2 Live ​

Changes since v0.3.1.

  • Loading on slow connections: files are now fetched a few at a time in the order the loaders need them (before, 100+ requests at once shared the bandwidth, so the background, logo and first models finished last and the bar stayed at 0).
  • The landing page's Play Now button opens /demo/ (latest), which is now republished with every release.

v0.3.1 ​

Changes since v0.3.0.

  • Loading: the page no longer reloads itself in the middle of loading on a slow connection (a 14 s watchdog did that), and loading is faster: every model and picture is requested at once while the loaders run one after another.
  • Booster buttons are bigger (82 px).

v0.3.0 ​

Changes since v0.2.0.

  • Level start, in order: the LEVEL words come first, the UI (top row, Backup, boosters) slides in while the bubble flies and has landed when it reaches the badge (the badge swells and wobbles), the first-time pop-up comes last. Hard (purple) and Super Hard (red-pink) levels from level 10 colour the words, the bubble and the number, with fish bones on the words. The words, the cheers and LEVEL COMPLETE! are painted pastel jelly lettering that ripples like water and is wrung into a spiral at the end.
  • Boosters at the bottom, bigger (82 px), in fixed slots (Hint, Clear Tank, Clear Backup). From level 2 all three show; a locked one is grey with a padlock and the level that opens it (4 / 7 / 9). Their first-time board sits above the button with a pointer; Backup fades with the boosters while the camera moves (and stays while a fish is still flying in).
  • Six looks, one per 10 levels: new 3D halls, water colours that differ more, curtains in the look's colours, pipe colour per look; the pipe fades as one block.
  • Combo cheers NICE! / GREAT! / SUPER! / WOW! (smaller, no x-count), a new LEVEL COMPLETE! title, the finished-tank swirl twists the whole tank and its plate; the win swirl hides the other fish so the orbiting ones are seen.
  • Target tanks: narrower, 3D water; a landing fish makes droplets that stay in the tank and leave rings. Pop-up headers curve with the ribbon; New feature pop-ups animate and show live pictures of the obstacles; the pipe message uses the cream board.
  • Loading screen and logo: own look (3D aquarium picture, the glossy Spot A Fish logo, a swimming 3D fish on the game's gauge, soft bubbles); it can no longer hang after a reload.
  • Fixes: effects stay inside the game frame, the lose blur covers the target tanks, Clear Tank is no longer wrongly grey, fish no longer hang nose-down under decor, the waltz event faces heads together, dev tools (one tap on the version: Levels, Reset, Win, Lose).
  • Known: the pop-up pictures from Bubble Fish are still temporary placeholders; the target tank glass picture is the old one.

v0.2.0 ​

Changes since v0.1.0.

  • 60 levels (about 2 h of play) built from the level design sheet, with a win-rate bot (game/tools/wr-sim.mjs) used to tune each level's difficulty. New obstacles and events: pipe, swarm, squid, clam, bounty fish; events now wait 8-10 s for each other.
  • New UI: Settings, Play On, Lose and first-time pop-ups (boosters and new features) redrawn from painted pieces, with pop-in/out animation; a fixed phone-size screen; booster buttons are bigger. Dev tools (Levels / Reset) show after tapping the version text 10 times.
  • Tutorial: boards for "Tap a fish" and the camera steps with animated hands (the board no longer jumps up and down). Levels with a first-time pop-up show the whole UI first, then the pop-up, then "LEVEL N".
  • Fish: all fish are 3D models, plus 12 new Tripo fish (test level 61); the new fish blink (10 of 12). Fixed the garbled colours of recoloured fish.
  • Fixes: pipe soft-lock when the tank is full of decoys; the level-start bubble moving behind pop-ups; Reset works with one tap.
  • Known: the pop-up pictures are temporary placeholders and will be replaced with new art; golden_spot, golden_tide and brown_stripe do not blink.

v0.1.0 ​

  • Backup tank cracks and breaks apart: before it bursts the glass shows a web of cracks that grows outwards from one impact point (inside the tank only). When it bursts the tank itself is cut into jagged pieces of its own picture that fly out and fall, instead of fading away.
  • Round bubbles in the tanks: the bubbles in the target and backup tanks were drawn as white squares; they are now round bubble rings.
  • Water effects rebuilt in 3D / shader: a tap now sends a pressure wave through the tank (a see-through shell lit at its rim plus two thin rings, hidden by fish in front). In the lose sequence the backup glass breaks into many irregular tumbling shards with bright edges, the fish are shot at the screen in about 0.2 s, and each hit splashes water on the glass right away. The water on the glass is a small thin-film simulation lit by a shader (rim, caustic, highlight): a hit leaves a thick ragged patch with tongues, gravity drains it (thick water runs fast, thin water hardly moves), the glass holds the water at some spots so the streams wind and split, and a sliding fish drags water along. Fish stay stuck about a second before sliding. The tank and background stay frosted (blurred) until the lose popup appears at 3.7 s, and the popup itself is sharp; the tap wave is thinner, fainter and fades in half a second.
  • Art folder assets in the game: the hint pointer above highlighted fish is now Arrow.png (pops in, bobs, squashes, sways, sparkles); combo cheers are the NICE / GREAT / EPIC / WOW pictures (2 / 3-4 / 5-6 / 7+ in a row) with a 3D tilt-in, thickness and a coloured burst, shown where the fish was tapped. Files live in public/ui/.
  • Backup tank polish: the grey bar under the backup tank is gone; it shakes less when nearly full and shows a soft red 3D rim that pulses gently (fades a red copy of the glass rim over the real one, with a red bloom). In the lose sequence the stuck fish are spread over the screen (one per cell of a grid, different sizes), the splashes are see-through splashes with fingers and droplets, and the drips are thin streams with a droplet head.
  • Backup tank recoloured and a better lose sequence: the backup tank is now deep ocean blue (same family as the target tanks) with a cyan rim and small gold corner accents. When it bursts, the fish that hit the screen now stick to it, wobble, then slide down leaving droplets while the screen glass turns wet with splashes, running drips and a growing frosted blur; the lose screen appears as the blur clears.
  • Top row centred: the level badge and the pause button are the same size, so the progress tube and its count sit exactly on the centre line (the gap between the two target tanks). The fish icon on the label plates is bigger and centred with the count.
  • Painted label plate and progress tube: the target label (fish icon and count) is now a small glossy glass plate that hangs on the tank's top rim with two brackets, and the progress gauge is a painted glass tube with the green water inside. Both were made in the asset workflow (originals in Added Assets/ui/, game copies in public/ui/).
  • Tank clear fix: the finishing tank's animation is now its own effect, so the next target no longer cancels it (the orb used to stay stuck and the gauge never filled). It is faster and livelier: wind-up squash, a ring of bubbles, swirl, a pop when the orb appears, a weaving flight with droplets and sparkles, a splash and gauge flash on arrival. The backup tank sits a little higher, closer to the target tanks.
  • Tank clear animation reworked for the painted tanks: the tank no longer spins like a card. The water picture swirls down like a drain (the fish swirl with it), the tank squeezes into the orb picture, and the orb flies up to the gauge stretching like a blob of jelly with a trail of droplets, then splashes into the gauge.
  • Painted tanks: the tanks now use painted pictures (water, glass and an orb) instead of plain 3D blocks: back picture, fish, front glass, a light shimmer and bubbles, tilted slightly so they read as 3D. The backup tank has its own violet / gold look with seven compartments. The finished tank's bubble is the orb picture. The pictures are in public/ui/tanks/ (originals in Added Assets/tanks/); how they were made is in the developer guide (Asset workflow).
  • Public repo guard: npm run check:public (also run by the release script, and by the optional npm run hooks:install pre-commit hook) blocks links to private tools, tokens, personal paths and e-mail addresses.
  • 3D fish colour variants: a 3D model can now have colour variants (the atlas is hue-turned on demand, same mesh and bones). Level 16 uses them: 5 targets in 5 evenly spaced colours, 3 decoy colours in between, and 1 look-alike of a target; 30 fish. The target and backup tanks are slimmer and the top UI takes about 28% of the screen height instead of about 40%.
  • 3D tanks: the two target tanks and the backup are now real 3D glass tanks drawn in the overlay (blue water, sand, plants, bubbles). The backup tank is different on purpose: violet water, gold frame, brass base and seven compartments; it shakes when it is nearly full. A finished target tank whirls, shrinks into a bubble, flies up and fills the progress gauge (the gauge fills when the bubble arrives). When the backup overflows it cracks and bursts: glass shards and water fly, the fish are shot toward the screen and splash on it, the game stalls for a moment, then the lose screen shows. The goal gauge also had its fill poking out of the rounded end, which is fixed.
  • UI clean-up: no frame or rivets on the top row, no corner brackets / base / chains / rivets on the target tanks, no brass rail under the backup jars, no camera up / down buttons. The goal gauge has no stars and shows the count in its middle. The deep navy panels are now a fresher, brighter blue. The camera tutorial's last step now teaches a two-finger drag up / down (Shift-drag or right-drag on desktop) instead of the arrow buttons.
  • Menu lag: while a menu or popup is open and the world is frozen, the 3D picture is no longer redrawn.
  • Level 16 bigger fish: 40 fish at once and the fish are 30% bigger (sizeK 1.3); 3D fish also swim about 1.45× faster, never hang still and pick farther swim targets.
  • Performance (3D fish): the model's 8 meshes and 121 bone nodes are merged at load into 1 mesh with 14 bones per fish (1 draw call and 1 skeleton instead of 8), fish use a cheaper lit material, and the fish mesh is indexed (630 vertices instead of 3078 per fish), the invisible tap-hit spheres are no longer drawn, and the water surface grid is 60×60 (level 16: about 59k triangles, 38k vertices). The render resolution steps down by itself (2 → 1.5) only when the frame rate stays under 36 fps, and steps back up after 12 s of 60 fps. ?hq=1 stops the automatic step-down; ?res=2 pins the render scale (3D and UI); ?res=3 renders at the phone's full pixel density. Test switches for slow phones: ?aa=0 (no edge smoothing), ?lens=1 / ?lens=0 (glass bending on / off; it is off by default on touch screens, where it looked softer and gained no speed). The ?fps=1 meter also shows the GPU time of the 3D canvas when the browser allows it. On touch screens the Phaser UI is redrawn about 30 times a second (?ui30=0 turns that off). The small fish in the target boards and backup tray are also drawn at 30 per second except while a fish is flying. ?res= only affects the 3D picture; ?uires=3 sets the UI's pixel density (default 2). Adding &prof=1 shows tap buttons that switch parts of the picture off (fish, water, rays, glass, decor, particles, UI, overlay, background) to see what each costs. The invisible second 3D canvas no longer uses edge smoothing on touch devices.
  • FPS meter: open the game with ?fps=1 to show the frame rate and slowest frame; window.__fps always has the latest numbers.
  • Dash swell and dash-spin (3D fish): a sudden dash pushes the water aside from where the fish launched: a short refraction swell bends the view of the tank (plus a few specks thrown outward); it needs the glass lens to be active. A 3D fish sometimes crouches, shoots off fast weaving left-right and up-down while rolling round its body, with rings radiating along the way.
  • Swim wake: swimming fish leave pale water specks behind the tail; faster fish leave more and brighter ones.
  • 3D fish animation: curved swimming with circling loops, turns with inertia and banking, a travelling body wave and bend into turns, moving fins, and eye expressions (blink, wide when hovered or startled, sleepy when hanging still).
  • 3D fish models (test): a rigged .fbx fish (Blue white babe) can replace the 2D fish. Test level L16 uses only this model, 40 fish. Fix: swarm fish no longer show square card backgrounds. Imported from the Find Fish prototype (changes since the first playtester feedback):
  • Playtest feedback: about 50–60% fewer decoy fish; fish per tank rises 1 → 2 → 3; slower, bigger fish early; long tutorial replaced by a goal popup and a pointing hand; level 1 cannot be lost; gentler stars.
  • Fish look and motion: softer outline from the fish's own colour, less glow, more body wobble and wider tail, longer swim paths, fewer idle fish.
  • Rewards: combo words with sparkles; every 3rd combo refills Hint.
  • Random events: squabble, conga, waltz, swirl, hide, pearl, startle (falling object), feeding.
  • Introductions by highlight: Backup (L2), Hint (L4), Clear Backup (L5); camera tutorial at L1.
  • Obstacles: squid moved to L3, clam to L6; bounty tanks from L6; fish swarm from L9.
  • Pump tube (L7+) and a fish cap (now 40 at once, also enforced for pump waves), with many display fixes so the tube is not bent by the lens.
  • Global collision between fish, obstacles, decorations and tube.
  • Feeding: 3D food bucket replaces the 3D hand.
  • Water surface: smooth normals, reflections rebuilt, grid reduced for performance.
  • Hover: double-layer gold outline on desktop.
  • Pause menu: Reset progress.
  • Project: moved into the docs and release structure (Three.js game kept).
  • Phaser 4 UI layer: the top bar, target tanks (small live aquariums), tool buttons, Backup tray, popups, spotlight tips and messages are drawn by Phaser above the 3D tank. Level editor added.
  • Clear Backup: returning fish now fly to the open top of the tank and drop in through it, with a splash, instead of appearing under the water.
  • Glass rim and tank shell: the water lens no longer bends the glass wall, rims, stand or plinth; only what is inside the water (fish, sand, decorations, surface) is refracted.
  • One event at a time: feeding, the pearl game, the falling rock, the swarm and tube waves now wait for each other instead of overlapping.
  • Fixes: Clear Backup no longer freezes the game when the Backup holds obstacles (they have no tail, which the new drop animation assumed). Dropping fish now dive head first, seen in profile, and carry on in the direction they dived. The gold hover outline follows the fish silhouette again.
  • UI style: brass and glass. The whole Phaser UI is drawn like aquarium equipment: a brass control rail with a porthole for the level and a valve wheel for pause; a glass water gauge that fills as target tanks are finished, marked with starfish (the stars of the game); hanging brass nameplates and corner brackets on the target tanks; a brass shelf with glass jars for the Backup (slimmer than before); brass-bezel tool buttons; popups in riveted brass frames with a coral banner and pearl buttons; a level list as a reef of pearls joined by stepping stones, with starfish for the stars; messages and spotlight tips with brass edges.
  • Hover glow: the gold highlight is now a glow hull around the whole 3D fish, so it wraps the fish from any angle (before it was a flat plane that showed only as a slice when the fish faced the camera).
  • Fish bodies: each fish is one closed, welded surface that follows the true outline of its picture, with a rounded edge, so there is no gap between the two faces. The rim is rounded with several rows of vertices following a circular profile, and vertices near the edge read the picture a few pixels inside, so there is no seam, stitch marks or pale bands even when a fish is seen head-on. The dark outline is a thin inverted hull behind the body, and the fish colours are smeared a few pixels past the outline, so edge-on views show no black tear or dots. Clear Backup fish leave the tray with a short hop and enter below it through the mouth of the tank. Detailed history is in the original project's CHANGELOG.md. Play latest
  • Level editor: a Tank preview panel runs the real game with the level being edited, and a list shows the fish species in the game.
